Distributed systems don’t always fail because something is completely down.  
  
Sometimes the most dangerous failure is a service that’s still alive… just slow enough to hurt everything around it.  
  
Requests pile up.  
Threads block.  
Retries add more load.  
Timeouts spread.  
And suddenly one slow dependency becomes a cascading failure.  
  
That’s why resilient systems need more than “add more servers.”  
  
Think: timeouts, circuit breakers, bounded retries, backpressure, load shedding, and isolation.  
  
The key idea:  
  
Failure is not binary. Design for degraded behavior, not just uptime.  
  
Save this if you’re preparing for system design interviews.
